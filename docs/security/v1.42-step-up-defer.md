# v1.42: approvals as grants, STEP_UP and DEFER

Status: built, released as v1.42.0 (the release PR, November 26, 2026; the 24-hour soak's results follow when it ends). Started November 25, 2026, after v1.41.0's release; design decided November 23, those decisions marked **(decided)**. Built in the steps of the implementation plan at the end, one PR each.

## Why

VAREK decides each mediated call ALLOW or DENY; an undecidable call (UNKNOWN) is refused. The AARM specification's Core level (`docs/security/aarm-mapping.md`) needs three more decisions:
- **STEP_UP:** a person approves the action before it runs.
- **DEFER:** the action waits for the context it needs.
- **MODIFY:** the action runs changed.

STEP_UP and DEFER are the gap that blocks Core: AARM's R4, and its DEFER clauses in R1, R3, R5 and R6. A general MODIFY is out of scope here (see the end).

AARM's model of STEP_UP is a person answering yes or no while the action waits. The spec names its costs:
- **approval fatigue:** people stop reading what they approve;
- **latency:** the agent waits on a person;
- **approvers who are deceived or coerced.**

Under that model, a "yes" is also opaque. Nothing checks afterwards that what ran was what the approver meant, or that the approval reached no further than intended.

## The design in one paragraph

**An approval is a grant: a small, signed, scoped rule, not a verdict.** A person approves the narrowest rule that would allow the action: this object, this operation, this many times, until this time, for this user, agent or sub-agent. The approved call is then decided by the machine against the policy plus the grant: the same SMT procedure, and the same certificate the independent checker verifies. So every ALLOW still carries a checked certificate, approvals included. The Warden decides on the object the kernel resolved, so an approver who was shown one thing can't authorise another. The audit replays every approval: each call within its grant, and each grant within the policy's `step_up` rule.

Keeping VAREK's human out of the loop (HOOTL), people approve when they write the policy and before the run where they can: a plan approved once, standing grants approved ahead of time. At run time, by default, the agent is never made to wait on a person. Holding the action for an answer, as AARM's test expects, is still available.

## How VAREK's properties carry into approvals

| VAREK's property | In this design |
|---|---|
| Human out of the loop | People decide when writing the policy, not while the agent runs. Plans are approved once, standing grants ahead of time, and runtime grants arrive asynchronously by default. |
| Every ALLOW carries a checked certificate | An approved call is decided by the SMT procedure against the policy plus the grant, and its certificate is verified. An approval is never an exception to the proof. |
| The three-state verdict | UNKNOWN maps to DEFER: held or refused, never let through. |
| Decided on the object delivered | The grant names the object; the Warden decides on the one the kernel resolved, so a swap between the request and the approval fails the grant. |
| Kernel enforcement, fail-closed | Holds and timeouts happen at the kernel. A timeout refuses, held calls are bounded, and the agent dies with its Warden. |
| The signed, hash-chained stream | Each grant, its approver and each use of it are records in the chain. |
| The independent audit | It replays every approval: the call within the grant, the grant within its `step_up` rule, the approver allowed, the signature valid. |
| The on-behalf-of chain and sub-agents | A grant can be scoped to a user, agent or sub-agent, as the kernel's control groups attribute it, not as the agent says. |
| Dry run and `policy suggest` | They synthesise the narrowest grant to propose. |
| The Defense build | Approvals need no network (local approvers, the run's socket), and grants are signed through the FIPS module. |

## Where approval happens, in order of preference

1. **Approve the plan, before the run.**
   - Under `--hold-to-plan`, a person approves the action-graph once, with full context; every call is then checked against it by machine.
   - `varek plan approve FILE` signs the plan; `--require-approved-plan` refuses a run whose plan an allowed approver hasn't signed.
   - A call outside the plan becomes *amend and re-approve the plan* (`varek plan extend RUN FILE`), not a series of runtime prompts.
2. **Standing grants, approved ahead of time.** `varek run --grants DIR` gives the run grants signed in advance, for example "the payments API, up to 5 calls today, for alice". Each is checked like a policy at the start. The human decision was made at writing time, and the run never waits.
3. **Runtime grants** for what neither covered, under a `step_up` rule, in one of two modes:
   - **`mode=grant`** (the default, keeping the human out of the loop): the call is refused at once, with `STEP_UP` recorded, and a proposed grant is posted to the approvers. If approved, the grant joins the run, and the agent's retry is decided against it with a certificate. The agent never waits.
   - **`mode=hold`** (AARM's STEP_UP): the call is held at the kernel until the grant is approved or refused, or the timeout refuses it. AARM's conformance test ("STEP_UP pauses") uses this mode.

## Grants

```
# a grant (signed): what one approval allows
grant 2f9c1e...
  step_up line 14                      # the policy's step_up rule it falls under
  allow connect host api.payments.example port 443
  for user alice@example.test subagent=billing
  count 3 until 2026-11-24T17:00:00Z
  approver kdouglas (group varek-approvers)
```

- **Grammar.** A grant body is one or more `allow` rules in the policy grammar (`require warden 1.42`), plus:
  - **scope:** `for user|agent|subagent=...`, and `run=ID` for a runtime grant;
  - **limits:** `count N` and `until TIME`;
  - **the `step_up` rule** it falls under.
- **The envelope.** A grant allows a call only if its own rules *and* its `step_up` rule both match. So a grant can't reach past what the policy marked for approval, nor widen the policy. The plan gate's checks, deny rules and the certificate checker apply as to any rule.
- **Proposals.** For a held or refused call, VAREK proposes the narrowest grant from the resolved object, as `varek policy suggest` does: the exact path or host and port, the operation, `count 1`, a short `until`, and the call's own `obo` scope. The approver accepts it or narrows it, never widens it: a grant wider than the proposal's `step_up` envelope is refused.
- **Signing.** Every grant record is signed in the stream by the Warden, with the approver's identity.
  - **Standing grants and plan approvals** must also be signed by the approver's own key: ECDSA P-384, from `varek_keygen`, pinned in the policy with `approver KEYFILE group=GROUP` (refined in step 6: `approver key=HEX group=GROUP [name=NAME]`, the key pinned by value). Nobody is present at the socket when they are made.
  - **Runtime grants** may be signed that way too (`varek approve --sign-key`). Otherwise the kernel's peer credentials identify the approver.
- **Use.** Each call a grant allows is recorded with the grant's id. When its count is used up or its time is past, the grant allows nothing more.

## DEFER

DEFER holds or refuses a call whose decision lacks context, then decides it again:
- **a call that is UNKNOWN** (outside the decidable fragment): `on unknown defer timeout=N` (the default stays `on unknown deny`);
- **a call no plan step covers,** under `--hold-to-plan`: `on outside_plan defer timeout=N`. A plan extension, approved and checked by the plan gate, resolves it (`varek plan extend`; no `escalate`, step 5b);
- **a rule that names its condition:** `defer ... until=FILE` waits for a marker, such as a ticket or a change window, to exist and pass the rule's check.

What resolves a deferral is context, decided by the machine, so DEFER is HOOTL already. `escalate` turns a deferral that context can't resolve into a STEP_UP, in the rule's mode. Otherwise it is refused at its timeout.

## Bounds, timeouts and order

- **Timeouts always refuse.** Each held call or proposed grant has a timeout: default 300 s, at most 3,600 s. There is no fail-open, and if the Warden stops, the agent is killed, as today.
- **`max_held N`** (default 8) caps the calls held at once in a run. One more is refused at once (AARM R4). `max_pending N` caps proposed grants waiting for an answer.
- **Order:**
  - A held call blocks only its own thread, so a later call that depends on it waits too.
  - Other threads go on, as AARM says independent actions should.
  - A dependency across threads is invisible to VAREK. `scope=run` holds the whole run instead: the run's control group is frozen (cgroup v2, `cgroup.freeze`) while the call is held, and thawed when the hold ends, its timeout included. The Warden is outside that group and goes on serving. On a host that can't freeze, a call that needs `scope=run` is refused, not held, so it fails closed. Such a host has no delegated cgroup v2 subtree, or a kernel before 5.19, where the freeze would end the held call's own wait (step 4). The hold's record names the scope applied.

  **(decided)** The defaults follow AARM:
  - **A held DEFER uses `scope=run`.** AARM's R4 says a dependent action "MUST also be deferred". Since VAREK can't see the dependency, it treats every thread as dependent. Deferrals are resolved by context, without a person, so the pause is short. `scope=thread` is there for an author who knows the threads are independent; AARM's SHOULD that independent actions proceed is then met.
  - **A held STEP_UP uses `scope=thread`.** AARM puts no dependency requirement on STEP_UP, and a person's answer takes longer, so freezing the whole agent costs more. `scope=run` is there where the threads share state.
  - **`mode=grant` holds nothing,** so scope doesn't apply: a refused call produces nothing for another call to depend on.

## Approvers

- **Commands:**
  - `varek pending` lists a run's held calls and proposed grants. Each shows the action and its resolved object, the rule and policy line, `obo`, the plan step, the run's recent decisions, the user's request (`--intent`) and the time left.
  - `varek approve RUN ID [--narrow ...] [--sign-key KEY] [--reason TEXT]` and `varek deny RUN ID` answer through the run's control socket (`/run/varek/<run>.ctl`, root's, as `varek revoke` uses).
- **Who may approve:** the Unix user making the request, from the socket's peer credentials, which the kernel vouches for. `approvers=GROUP` on the `step_up` rule limits approval to that group, so root alone is not an approver by default.

  **(decided in step 2, November 25)**
  - A rule without `approvers=` is approved by root alone, as `varek revoke` is.
  - The agent's own user is never an approver, even in the group. So an agent run as root (`--run-as root`) can have nothing approved.
  - A user sees only the proposals they may answer, so other local users don't learn the run's objects.

  **(decided)** A Unix group in v1.42, and an identity provider's group later:
  - peer credentials need no network, so approvals work air-gapped and in FIPS mode;
  - an identity provider's group, verified as v1.28.1 verifies a user's token, can follow, with an approval channel for the Kubernetes node agent.
- **Notification:** `notify=URL` posts each proposed grant to a webhook (Slack, Teams, a ticketing system). The post holds the grant's text, so the approver sees exactly what they would authorise, plus the run, the rule, for whom and until when. It holds none of the call's data beyond the grant's object. The webhook only notifies; it can't approve. A failed post is recorded and retried once, and nothing else depends on it.

  **(decided)** In v1.42: the paper's R4 says the system "MUST route approval requests to configured approvers".
- **Fatigue:** plan approval and standing grants are the main tools. Runtime grants are for the rare remainder. `step_up` belongs on destructive writes, payments and data leaving, as AARM advises.

## The user's original request (`--intent`)

**(decided)** In v1.42. `varek run --intent FILE` (or `--intent-text TEXT`) records the user's request, as the agent framework received it, in `run_start`: its SHA-256 and the text up to 4 KiB, or the hash and a file beside the log. It is shown to approvers and carried in the export.

It is context for people, not for the decision: no rule matches on it, so a request written to persuade can't change what is allowed. It closes the part of AARM's R2 that asks for the original request.

## The kernel side

- **A held call** is a seccomp notification the Warden hasn't answered; the kernel keeps the thread waiting.
- **Signals:** with `SECCOMP_FILTER_FLAG_WAIT_KILLABLE_RECV` (Linux 5.19 and later), only a fatal signal ends the wait. On older kernels a signal can end it, and the call fails with `EINTR` without effect. Either way it is recorded as `abandoned`. The freezer stops a thread with a signal's wake-up, so `scope=run` needs the flag (step 4).
- **Before answering,** the Warden checks the notification is still valid (`SECCOMP_IOCTL_NOTIF_ID_VALID`).
- **The egress proxy** holds a network request or MCP tool call the same way, not forwarding it.
- **The Warden goes on serving** other notifications; held calls live in a table.

## The record and the audit (AARM R5 and R6)

- **New records:**
  - `STEP_UP` or `DEFER` (with `held` or `proposed`, the reason and the timeout);
  - `grant` (the grant's text, its id, the approver, the signatures and the time);
  - the ending (`approved`, `denied`, `resolved`, `timeout` or `abandoned`);
  - each use: the decision record names the grant's id.

  All carry the original call's `obo` and are chained and signed like every record.
- **The audit (`varek_audit.py`) checks:**
  - every hold and proposal has exactly one ending, within its timeout;
  - every grant falls within its `step_up` rule, with an approver from its group and valid signatures (the approver's key, pinned in the policy, where one is required);
  - every call allowed under a grant matches it, within its scope, count and time, and carries a certificate the checker accepts against the policy plus the grant;
  - no grant is used after it ends;
  - a forged, missing, widened or overused grant fails the run's audit.
- **The export:** `STEP_UP`, `DEFER` and grants appear in OCSF and in the Splunk, Sentinel and OpenTelemetry exports. Sentinel gains a rule for expired approvals.

**What remains trusted:** the approver is a principal who can be deceived or coerced. But an approver can only authorise a precise grant within the policy's `step_up` envelope, which the machine then checks. The threat model records the approver, the envelope and the grant check.

## Not in v1.42

- **A general MODIFY.** Rewriting a system call's arguments risks a time-of-check race. A narrow form, such as dropping an MCP call's forbidden argument, is a candidate for later, kept to changes the checker can verify. Credential release (v1.28) stays the one MODIFY.
- **Semantic drift** (AARM R7).
- **Context across runtime calls** (a read of sensitive data refusing a later send): the cross-action data-flow candidate (v1.11).
- **Approvers from an identity provider,** and approval on Kubernetes.

## Tests (`make test-v1420`) and ship gate

- **Grants:**
  - a proposal is the narrowest rule;
  - approving, narrowing and refusing one;
  - a grant wider than its envelope refused;
  - count and time limits;
  - scope by user, agent and sub-agent;
  - standing grants checked at the start, and one with a bad or missing signature refused;
  - an approved plan, and `--require-approved-plan`.
- **Each decision:** ALLOW, DENY, STEP_UP in `mode=grant` (refused, then the retry allowed after approval) and `mode=hold` (held, then approved or refused), DEFER resolved by a plan extension or its marker, DEFER escalated, and UNKNOWN deferred. Each comes with its records and the audit passing.
- **Timeouts** refuse; `max_held` and `max_pending` hold; order within and across threads.
- **Robustness:**
  - a signal, the agent exiting, or the Warden killed during a hold;
  - an approver outside the group;
  - an answer for a call that has ended;
  - a symlink swapped between proposal and approval (the grant doesn't match);
  - `scope=run` freezing the run's other threads and thawing them, a timeout thawing them, and refusal where the host can't freeze.
- **The audit's forgeries:** a grant forged, widened, used beyond its count or time, without its approver or signature, or with its ending missing; an ALLOW under a grant without a certificate.
- **Latency:** calls not held are decided no slower than in v1.41 (`make latency-*`).
- **Ship gate:**
  - the suites and the cross-check;
  - a 24-hour soak in which a script approves a runtime grant every turn, a standing grant allows a call every turn, and a DEFER times out every tenth;
  - AARM's R1 to R6 conformance tests, in `mode=hold`, recorded in `docs/security/aarm-mapping.md`.

## Implementation plan

One PR for each step, merged to main in order, each with its tests. The release PR comes last. As for v1.35, a step may refine the design, and its PR says where.

1. **The grammar and the decision, in the procedure and in the checker.** This step touches no Warden code.
   - **Grammar,** after `require warden 1.42`:
     - `step_up` rules, with the same matchers as `allow` and `deny`, and `mode=`, `approvers=`, `timeout=`, `scope=` and `notify=`, last on the line;
     - `max_held` and `max_pending`.

     The DEFER lines come with step 5 and `approver KEYFILE group=GROUP` with step 6, each with the code that acts on it, so that no step accepts a line it does not yet carry out.
   - **Grant text:** its `allow` rules, `for`, `count`, `until` and the `step_up` line it falls under.
   - **The procedure** (`smt_decide.c`):
     - a call whose first holding rule is a `step_up` rule is UNSATISFIED, the deciding rule that `step_up` rule, so every caller refuses it until step 2. In the symbolic case, `step_up` is an outcome of its own: a mix with ALLOW or with DENY is UNKNOWN.
     - `vdp_decide_grant` decides a call within the grants under that rule. A grant's rules are decided as the policy's are, at most 8 of them, kept with the grant rather than in a policy of their own.
     - Lint counts a request rule over a host a `step_up` rule decides as one that can fire, since a grant may open the host.
   - **The certificate for an ALLOW under a grant is a pair:**
     - the `step_up` rule is the first rule of the policy holding on the object;
     - a rule of the grant is the first of the grant's rules holding on it.
   - **The checker** (`checker/vdp_checker.c`, its own parser) gets the same grammar, its own reading of a grant (`vdpc_grant_load`), and `vdpc_check_grant`, which verifies both halves. A path that a `step_up` rule decides counts as openable in the Warden's start-up check of its own files, since a grant may open it.
   - **The Warden** compares the two parsers' reading of every `step_up` option at load, as it does every other field, and resolves a `step_up` rule's host names with the deny rules'.
   - **Tests:** `vdp_check` and `vdp_cert_check` take grants (`batch GRANT...`, `grant GRANT`); `make test-v1420`'s section 1 covers every form, each refusal by both parsers, and the checker's refusal of each forged certificate. The cross-check's oracle (`tools/smt_crosscheck.py`) reads `step_up` rules and decides them exactly, and its fuzzing writes policies with them.
2. **Runtime grants in `mode=grant`** (built November 25, 2026; `warden_grant.inc.c`).
   - **In the Warden:** a `step_up` match is refused at once.
     - **The decision record** names the proposal (`"step_up":ID`), and a `step_up` record holds the proposed grant's text.
     - **The proposal is the narrowest grant:** the exact path, host or program, with an open's own access mode and `O_CREAT`, `O_TRUNC` and `O_APPEND`; `for run=`, and the call's user and sub-agent; `count 1`; until the rule's timeout.
     - **The same proposal is not made twice** while it is open, and at most `max_pending` are open at once.
   - **The approvers' socket:** `/run/varek-approve/<run>.sock`, mode 0666, in a directory 0711. Every request is the peer's, as `SO_PEERCRED` names it. `revoke` stays on root's socket, `/run/varek/<run>.ctl`. Its requests:
     - `pending`;
     - `approve ID` (optionally `reason TEXT`, and a narrowed grant's text);
     - `deny ID`.
   - **What an approval needs:**
     - **The grant** is read by both parsers.
     - **It is no wider than the proposal:** the same `step_up` rule, a count and a time no greater, this run, and the proposal's user and sub-agent.
     - **The record:** a `grant` record holds the text, its SHA-256 as the id, the approver and the reason, then `step_up_end` (`approved`).
   - **Other endings:**
     - `denied`;
     - `timeout`, at the rule's timeout;
     - `abandoned`, for a proposal still open when the run ends.
   - **The retry** is decided under the grant (`vdp_decide_grant`), and allowed only when the checker accepts the certificate pair (`vdpc_check_grant`). The decision records `cert_grant`, `cert_grant_rule` and `cert_grant_witness`, and a use is counted when the checker accepts.
   - **The rest:**
     - `varek pending`, `varek approve` (`--narrow FILE`, `--reason`) and `varek deny`.
     - **The audit's `GrantsCheck`:** every proposal named, recorded and ended once; each grant's id its text's SHA-256 and approving an open proposal; each use within the count, the time and the run; and each use's two halves re-checked by the checker against the grant's file.
     - **The proxy's host-name decisions** go through the same decide and certify, so grants and proposals apply to them.
   - **Left to later steps:**
     - **Proposals for requests and MCP calls,** with step 3b, where the proxy holds them. Until then such a call is refused, its record saying why no proposal was made.
     - **Options not built yet:** a policy that used `mode=hold`, `scope=run` or `notify=` was refused at start until its step was built (the last, `notify=`, in step 7).
3. **`mode=hold`, held at the kernel** (built November 25, 2026; `warden_grant.inc.c`).
   - **The holding:** a call a `mode=hold` rule refuses isn't answered.
     - **What's kept:** its notification and the call as decided. A grant is proposed, and a `step_up` record (`mode` `hold`, `state` `held`) names the call. There's no decision record yet.
     - **The mechanism:** both points every refusal passes through take part. `emit_pathology` holds the call instead of recording it, and `send_simple` and `send_errno` withhold its refusal. So no syscall handler changed.
     - **Only an unanswered call is held.** A held call whose refusal reached the kernel some other way is recorded then and ends `abandoned`.
   - **Approved:** the call is decided again from its notification, as the node agent's first call is (the supervision loop's `decide`), now within the grant.
     - The object the kernel resolves then must be the grant's. A symlink swapped while the call was held is refused, its record saying so, and no new proposal is made.
     - Allowed, it carries the certificate pair like any call under a grant.
   - **Denied or past the rule's timeout:** the held call is refused (EACCES), and its record names the proposal (`"held":true`) before `step_up_end`.
   - **Interrupted:** a notification that ends while held (the caller interrupted or gone) ends `abandoned`.
     - **With Linux 5.19 or later,** the filter asks the kernel to end the wait on a fatal signal only (`SECCOMP_FILTER_FLAG_WAIT_KILLABLE_RECV`). Step 3 asked through libseccomp 2.6. From step 4, the Warden loads the program an older libseccomp builds itself, with the flag.
     - **Without them,** the Warden says so at start, and an ordinary signal ends the wait. The call, notified again, is held again under a new proposal.
     - **Only when the policy holds calls** is the flag asked for, so no other run's calls change how signals reach them.
   - **Bounds:**
     - **`max_held`** (default 8): one more call to hold is refused at once, its record saying `max_held`.
     - **Ordering:** a held call blocks its own thread only. The supervision loop goes on serving the run's other calls.
     - **No approvers' socket:** no call is held, since nobody could answer it.
   - **What is held:** opens and lookups, connects and launches.
     - **Changes by name** decide two names, so a `mode=hold` rule refuses them at once with a proposal, as `mode=grant` does.
     - **Decided at the egress proxy:** so are the host names the proxy decides. Requests and MCP calls are held at the proxy (step 3b).
   - **Two related fixes:**
     - **The launch set** (Landlock) now holds what a `step_up` exec rule names, so an approved launch can run. The Warden still decides each launch.
     - **Writable rules:** a `step_up` rule that could write counts as writable, for the noexec mounts and the audit's check of them, since a grant may let the agent write there.
   - **The audit:**
     - a held call's `step_up` record precedes any decision on it;
     - a held call denied or timed out has its refusal recorded before its ending;
     - one approved or abandoned has none.
3b. **Holding at the egress proxy** (built November 25, 2026).
   - **The hold:** a request or MCP call a `mode=hold` rule refuses gets no verdict. The Warden keeps the proxy's report, proposes a grant and records `step_up` (`held`, with `proxy_conn`).
     - **The proxy is told** (`WP_MSG_HELDNOTE`) to wait for that connection's verdict for the hold's timeout plus 5 s, instead of `WP_VERDICT_MS` (60 s). Its next verdict ends that wait.
   - **Approved:** the report is decided again within the grant (`px_httpreq_decide`, `px_mcpreq_decide`, split from the checks made once on the report). The request or call is then sent on, under the grant's certificate pair.
   - **Denied or timed out:** it is refused (the client gets 403), its record naming the proposal (`"held":true`).
   - **Abandoned:** a connection that closes while held ends `abandoned`.
   - **Proposals:**
     - **For a request,** `allow request METHOD URL`.
     - **For an MCP call,** `allow mcp URL METHOD TOOL`.
     - **One rule for every kind:** a proposal is made only if the procedure confirms the grant allows the very call it is for. An object the grammar can't name exactly (a URL holding a glob's `*` or `[`, an MCP name holding `*` or `?`) gets none, and its record says why.
   - **Still decided at once:** a host name the proxy decides, under a `mode=hold` host rule, is refused at once with a proposal, as `mode=grant`.
4. **`scope=run`** (built November 25, 2026; `warden_grant.inc.c`, `warden_subagent.inc.c`).
   - **The run's control group:** a policy that holds calls with `scope=run` gets one, as sub-agents do.
     - With sub-agents named, it is theirs: each sub-agent's group is below it and freezes with it.
     - Otherwise it's made at the start: `varek-<run>` under the Warden's own group. The agent enters it before its launch.
     - The Warden and its helpers (the egress proxy, the resolver) stay outside it and go on serving.
   - **The freeze:** a call a `scope=run` rule holds, at the kernel or at the proxy, freezes the group (`cgroup.freeze`).
     - **The record:** `run_frozen` follows the hold's `step_up` record (`scope` `run`).
     - **What stops:** every other thread and process of the run, at its next return from the kernel. A call already in the kernel when the freeze began is decided as any.
   - **The thaw:** at the top of the supervision loop, once no call held with `scope=run` is left.
     - An approved call is decided again and answered first, so it finishes before the rest of the run goes on.
     - A refusal (denied or past the timeout) and an abandoned call thaw too.
     - **The record:** `run_thawed`, with `frozen_ms`. The run's end thaws before `run_end`.
   - **Freezing needs the held call to wait through it.** The freezer stops a thread with a signal's wake-up, which would end a held call's ordinary wait: the kernel would withdraw the notification and send it again, held again. So the run can be frozen only where the kernel ends a received notification's wait on a fatal signal only (`SECCOMP_FILTER_FLAG_WAIT_KILLABLE_RECV`, Linux 5.19 and later).
     - **The check:** a child tries it at the start.
     - **The filter:** loaded with the flag whatever libseccomp's version. Before 2.6, which cannot ask for it, the Warden loads the program libseccomp builds itself, with the flag (`load_waitkill`).
     - So step 3's holds wait through ordinary signals on every such kernel too.
   - **Where the run can't be frozen, it's never stopped.** A call a `scope=run` rule would hold is refused at once, with a proposal, as `mode=grant`. Its record says why (`unheld`: `scope=run: ...`), and `run_start` says which (`scope_run`: `freeze`, and the group or why not). The cases are:
     - no cgroup v2, or a group that can't be made (a read-only cgroup filesystem in a container);
     - a policy letting the agent write the control groups;
     - the agent running as root (`--run-as root`);
     - a kernel before 5.19.
   - **Writes to the cgroup filesystem:** while the run can be frozen, the Warden opens nothing there for writing on the agent's behalf, as with sub-agents.
   - **The audit, `GrantsCheck`:**
     - a hold with `scope=run` only where `run_start` says the run can be frozen;
     - `run_frozen` right after the hold that began it;
     - no `run_thawed` while such a hold is open, or before its approved call is decided again;
     - the run doesn't end frozen.
   - **Under the node agent** nothing is held (there's no approvers' socket), so nothing is frozen.
5. **DEFER: `defer ... until=FILE`, `on unknown defer` and `escalate`** (built November 25, 2026; `warden_grant.inc.c`). Split from 5b at Ken's choice (November 25, 2026), as 3 and 3b were.
   - **(decided, Ken, November 25, 2026)** Three choices:
     - **An UNKNOWN call can't become decidable by more context.** So `on unknown defer` is resolved only by `escalate` (a person's grant) or refused at its timeout. A plan extension doesn't resolve it.
     - **`until=FILE` is a root-owned marker.** FILE must exist as a regular file, owned by root or by the rule's `approvers=` group, and writable by its owner alone. Its first line names this run's id, or `*`. An optional `until YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` line bounds it. A policy that lets the agent write FILE is refused at start.
     - **DEFER is split:** 5 builds the marker, UNKNOWN and `escalate`; 5b builds `on outside_plan defer` with `varek plan extend`.
   - **The grammar,** after `require warden 1.42`, read by both parsers:
     - `defer KIND MATCHERS... until=FILE`, with the same matchers as `allow`, `deny` and `step_up`. The options come last: `until=` (an absolute path, required), `timeout=N` (1 to 3600; default 300), `scope=thread|run` (default `run`, decided above for a held DEFER), `escalate`, and `approvers=GROUP`.
     - `on unknown deny` (the default) or `on unknown defer escalate`, with `timeout=`, `scope=` and `approvers=`, at most once. `escalate` is required: nothing but a person can resolve an UNKNOWN call, and holding one with nothing to wait for would only delay its refusal.
   - **The decision:**
     - **A `defer` rule** is a conditional allow. The call is allowed under it while its marker passes, and deferred otherwise.
       - The procedure decides a call whose first holding rule is a `defer` rule UNSATISFIED, the deciding rule that rule. With symbolic flags, `defer` is an outcome of its own.
       - The Warden checks the marker. Where it passes, the call is allowed, its certificate's rule the `defer` rule.
       - The checker accepts a certificate for a `defer` rule when the Warden says the marker passed (`vdpc_check_defer`). The record carries the marker's path and SHA-256, so the audit can check it was one that passes.
     - **`on unknown defer escalate`** acts as a `step_up` rule after every rule of the policy, in `mode=hold`. It applies to a call no rule holds (UNKNOWN for want of a rule). A call UNKNOWN for another reason (past the length bound, unknown flags) is refused at once, since no grant could name it.
       - **The grant:** its `step_up line N` names the `on unknown` line.
       - **The certificate:** its step_up half is that no rule of the policy holds on the object (`vdpc_check_grant`, the half for line N of an `on unknown` line).
   - **The Warden:**
     - **A deferred call** is held at the kernel or at the proxy, as in steps 3, 3b and 4, under its scope. There's a `defer` record (`state` `held`, the marker's path), and `max_held` counts it.
     - **Each second while a deferral is open,** the marker is checked. When it passes, the held call is decided again and allowed under the rule, then `defer_end` (`resolved`, the marker's SHA-256).
     - **At its timeout:**
       - without `escalate`, refused (`defer_end` `timeout`);
       - with it, the deferral becomes a STEP_UP proposal, held on for the rule's timeout again (`defer_end` `escalated`, then `step_up` as in step 3).
     - **`on unknown defer escalate`** proposes the STEP_UP at once (`defer_end` `escalated`, with no wait), since no marker can resolve it.
   - **The audit:**
     - each deferral ends once;
     - a call allowed under a `defer` rule names a marker that passes;
     - an escalation's proposal follows its ending;
     - a grant under the `on unknown` line is re-checked by the checker as any other.
   - **As built (refinements):**
     - **The marker** must also be a regular file reached without a link (`O_NOFOLLOW`), at most 4 KiB, not owned by the agent's user. Its lines after the first are blank, `#` comments or one `until` line. A marker present before the call allows it at once: the record carries `cert_defer` and `defer_marker` (path, SHA-256), with no deferral.
     - **The records:**
       - `defer`: `id`, `state` `held`, `rule_line`, `until`, `timeout`, `scope`, `escalate`, the call, and `agent_pid` or `proxy_conn`; `"unknown":true` under the `on unknown` line.
       - `defer_end`: `ending` `resolved` (with `marker_sha256`), `timeout`, `escalated` (with the `step_up` proposal) or `abandoned`.
       - A deferred call's refusal names it (`"defer":ID,"held":true`), and so does its re-decision after `resolved` (`"defer":ID`). A call not deferred says why (`"defer":"none","defer_why":...`): not a call that can be held, `max_held`, or the run can't be frozen.
     - **Escalation:** an escalated deferral's proposal gets the rule's timeout again. At the proxy, the connection waits twice the timeout.
     - **A launch no rule names** isn't escalated under `on unknown`: the launch set (Landlock) holds only what rules name, so no grant could run it. A launch a `defer` rule decides is in the launch set, as a `step_up` rule's is.
     - **The approvers' socket** opens for a `defer` rule with `escalate` and for the `on unknown` line too. A deferral without `escalate` needs no socket.
     - **Freezing:** a deferral with `scope=run`, the default, freezes the run as a held `step_up` call does (step 4). Where the run can't be frozen, it's refused at once, its record saying why.
     - **The proxy** reads a grant under the `on unknown` line, which has no deciding rule, as one with no body limit and no credential.
5b. **`on outside_plan defer`** under `--hold-to-plan` (built November 25, 2026; `warden_grant.inc.c`, `tools/varek`).
   - **The grammar,** after `require warden 1.42`, in both parsers and the cross-check's oracle: `on outside_plan deny` (the default) or `on outside_plan defer`, with `timeout=N`, `scope=thread|run` (default `run`) and `approvers=GROUP`, at most once. The Warden compares the two readings at load. Not part of any decision.
   - **(decided)** `escalate` is refused on this line. The plan extension is the person's answer, and a grant can't make a plan cover a call. `varek pending` suggests the one-step extension instead.
   - **Deferred:** under `--hold-to-plan`, a call the policy allows but no plan step covers is held instead of refused (`outside_plan`).
     - It's held at the kernel or at the proxy, as in steps 3 and 3b; for a request, when it carries a credential no step names. `max_held` counts it, and `scope=run` freezes the run.
     - **The record:** `defer` with `"outside_plan":true`, `rule_line` the line, and `escalate` false.
     - **Where it can't be held** (a change by name, `max_held`, no freezing for `scope=run`, no approvers' socket), it's refused at once as before, its record saying why (`"defer":"none"`, `defer_why`).
     - **Without `--hold-to-plan`** the line is inert, and a note at start says so.
   - **`varek plan extend RUN PLAN`** sends `extend` and a plan's text (at most 16 KiB) over the approvers' socket.
     - **The peer** must be an approver of the line: its `approvers=` group, else root alone, never the agent's own user.
     - **The decision:** the Warden decides the plan with the basic plan gate (with `--flow-policy` too: a note at start says the flow policy isn't consulted for it). The steps of a plan it authorizes join the run's plan; a refused plan adds nothing.
     - **The record:** `plan_extended`, with the text's SHA-256, `actions`, `verdict`, the `entries` added and the approver. The answer is `extended SHA` or `refused WHY`.
     - **Released:** each held outside_plan call the extension covers ends `resolved` (`plan_extension`, the SHA-256), then is decided again and allowed as any covered call.
   - **`varek pending`** lists each such deferral to the line's approvers, with a one-step extension that covers it (`file_open` with `open=read` for a read, `net_connect`, or `process_exec`). Sent back as it is, the plan gate decides it.
   - **Endings:** at its timeout the call is refused (its record naming the deferral, `held`), then `defer_end` `timeout`. At the run's end, or when its caller is gone, `abandoned`.
   - **The audit:**
     - an outside_plan deferral never escalates;
     - it ends `resolved` only by an extension recorded before it with the verdict SATISFIED;
     - its call, decided again after that ending, is allowed with no marker;
     - each `plan_extended` record names its SHA-256, verdict and approver.
6. **Signed approvals** (built November 26, 2026; `warden_signed.inc.c`, `varek_approval.h`, `tools/varek_keygen.c`, `tools/varek`).
   - **The grammar,** after `require warden 1.42`, in both parsers and the cross-check's oracle: `approver key=HEX group=GROUP [name=NAME]`, the options in any order, each once. At most 16 lines, and the same key twice is refused. The Warden compares the two readings at load.
     - **(refined)** The key is pinned by value, not by a `KEYFILE` path: HEX is the approver's `KEY.pub` as `varek_keygen` writes it (the uncompressed P-384 point, 194 lowercase hex digits). So the policy's digest covers the keys themselves, and no file beside the policy can change who approves.
     - `name=` (`[A-Za-z0-9._-]`, 1 to 32) is recorded with each signature. `group=` is the Unix group the key's holder approves for.
   - **One signature format,** a detached file `X.sig`: `varek-approval 1`, `kind KIND`, `key HEX`, `sig SIGHEX`, four lines. The signature is ECDSA-P384-SHA384 (DER, as hex) over `varek-approval-v1` NUL KIND NUL and the exact bytes of X, with KIND `grant` or `plan`, so a grant's signature can't pass as a plan's. Made and checked only through `varek_fips`, the FIPS provider in the Defense build.
     - **`varek_keygen --sign KIND KEYFILE FILE [--force]`** writes FILE.sig; **`--verify KIND PUBHEX FILE`** exits 0 when it holds under that key.
   - **Standing grants:** `varek run --grants DIR` (the Warden's `--grants DIR`). Each `DIR/NAME.grant` with its `NAME.grant.sig` is checked at the start, and any that fails refuses the start (fail closed). Each must:
     - be read by both parsers, within its rule, as a runtime grant is;
     - be signed (kind `grant`) by a pinned key whose group is the `approvers=` group of the rule it falls under (or of the `on unknown` line). A rule without `approvers=` takes no standing grant: root alone approves it, at the socket;
     - name no run (`for run=`): a standing grant is for any run;
     - be within its `until`.

     Each is then live as an approved runtime grant is, its count and time used the same way. **The record:** `grant` with `"standing":true`, its file, `signed_by` (`key`, `name`, `group`), `sig` and its text, after `run_start` and before any decision.
   - **Plans:** `varek plan approve PLAN --key KEYFILE` writes PLAN.sig (kind `plan`). The Warden's `--require-approved-plan` (with `--plan`) refuses the start unless PLAN.sig holds, by any pinned key, over the very bytes the plan gate reads (its snapshot).
     - **Without the flag,** a PLAN.sig present is still checked: when it holds the run is approved; when it fails the run goes on unapproved, with a note.
     - **The record:** `run_start` carries `plan_approved_by` (`key`, `name`, `group`, `sig`, `plan_sha256`), or `plan_approval_note` saying why an approval present failed.
   - **Runtime grants:** `varek approve RUN ID --sign-key KEYFILE` signs the very text approved (the proposal's own, or the `--narrow` grant) and sends `sig SIGHEX key HEX` on the approvers' socket.
     - **The check:** the key must be pinned, and of the rule's `approvers=` group when it names one; the signature must verify over the grant's text. A signature that fails refuses the approval. The peer-credential checks apply as ever: a signature adds to who may approve, never replaces it.
     - **The record:** the `grant` record also carries `signed_by` and `sig`.
   - **Under the node agent,** `--grants` and `--require-approved-plan` are refused.
   - **The audit** reads the approver keys and each rule's `approvers=` group from the certificate checker (`vdp_cert_check POLICY approvers`), and verifies every signature with the `cryptography` package over the same message bytes:
     - a standing grant approves no proposal, is signed by a pinned key of its rule's group, names no run and was recorded within its `until`;
     - a runtime grant with `signed_by` is signed by a pinned key, of the rule's group when it names one;
     - `plan_approved_by` names a pinned key with the policy's name and group for it, and the plan the run is held to. With `--plan` its signature is verified over the plan's bytes; without, the report says it couldn't be;
     - a forged, swapped or missing signature fails the audit.
7. **`--intent` and `notify=URL`** (built November 26, 2026; `warden_notify.inc.c`, `warden_notify_helper.c`, `warden_notify.h`, `tools/varek`). Ken's direction that day was to choose the best options; the choices are below, with the two places where the code changed them.
   - **The user's request:** `varek run --intent FILE`, or `--intent-text TEXT` (written to a private file for the run). The Warden's `--intent FILE` reads it at the start: a regular file, not reached through a symlink at its last name, at most 1 MiB, else the start is refused.
     - **The record:** `run_start`'s `intent` holds its `sha256` and `bytes`, with its `text` when it is UTF-8 of at most 4 KiB. Otherwise it holds `file`: `STREAM.intent`, beside the verdict stream (the Warden's stderr, which must then be a regular file), made anew, root's, 0600.
     - **Shown:** `varek pending` shows it above the proposals: its first 2 KiB, cut at a character's start and marked `truncated`. It goes only to a peer who may answer something open in the run, and to no one else.
     - **Context only:** no rule matches on it, and it isn't in the webhook's post. Under the node agent `--intent` is refused.
   - **`notify=URL`,** on a `step_up` rule: `https://` with any host, or (**refined**) `http://` to a literal loopback address only, `127.0.0.0/8` or `[::1]` with a port from 1 to 65535, for a local relay. A name such as `localhost` isn't taken, since what it resolves to is the host's to say. The grammar is in both parsers and the cross-check's oracle. A URL with user information (`@`) is refused at start.
   - **Who posts (refined):** `warden-notify`, a helper of its own, as `warden-proxy` and `warden-secrets` are. The plan was a child of the Warden; two things in the code ruled that out. The Warden links no TLS code, and its supervision loop stops on any child's `SIGCHLD`.
     - **Started at the start,** before the agent, from a sealed copy whose SHA-256 is in `run_start` (`notify`: `helper_sha256`, `uid`, `gid`). It is not the Warden's child.
     - **Its user:** `--notify-as` (default `varek-notify` where that user exists, else 65530:65530), never the agent's, the proxy's or `warden-secrets`'. It has no capabilities and no new privileges, and isn't dumpable. `--notify-bin` names it; a startup check finds and hashes it.
     - **Each post** runs in a child of the helper, so posts never wait on each other and never block supervision. Each attempt (connect, TLS, send and the answer's status line) has 10 s. One failed attempt (no connection, TLS refused, no answer, or an answer other than 2xx) is made again once, 5 s later. No redirect is followed.
     - **TLS:** verified against the host's trust store (OpenSSL's default paths; `SSL_CERT_FILE` and `SSL_CERT_DIR` are passed on from the Warden's environment). The URL's host is checked against the certificate, as a name with SNI, or as an address. In the Defense build the helper runs on the FIPS provider, as `warden-secrets` does.
   - **The post:** JSON, `Content-Type: application/json`. Its `text` is one line, which Slack's and Teams' incoming webhooks show. Its `varek` holds `run`, `id`, `rule_line`, `mode`, `approvers`, `grant` (the proposal's exact text), `user`, `subagent`, `until` and `expires`. Nothing of the call goes in beyond the grant's object.
   - **The record:** one `notify` per post, after its proposal's `step_up` record (and `run_frozen`, if any). It holds `id`, `rule_line`, `host` and `url_sha256`, and never the URL, since a Slack URL is itself a secret. The outcome is `sent` (`status`, `attempts`), `failed` (`why`, `attempts`, the last `status`) or `abandoned`, when the run ends first. At the run's end the helper's socket closes and its posts still being made are ended.
     - **A policy with a secret URL** must not be readable by the agent. The policy isn't a secret store, and the records keep only the URL's digest.
     - **Nothing else depends on the outcome.** Proposals under a `defer` rule's `escalate` and the `on unknown` line aren't posted, since neither takes `notify=`.
   - **Under the node agent,** `notify=` is refused.
   - **The audit** reads each rule's URL from the checker (`vdp_cert_check POLICY notify`):
     - each proposal of a `notify=` rule has exactly one `notify` outcome (by the run's end, when the stream is complete), after its `step_up` record, and no other proposal has one;
     - its line, host and URL digest are the rule's, and its outcome one the Warden writes: `sent` with a 2xx status in 1 or 2 attempts;
     - `run_start` names `warden-notify` when the policy has `notify=`;
     - the request's SHA-256 and length match its text, or the file beside the stream, and no other record holds it.
